What about blackberry though? We totally forgot about that
Fair — I glossed over it. Real answer: citrus-tone isn't ideal for blackberry, but it's not wrong either.
The issue: Blackberries want more potassium and less phosphorus than a citrus formula gives. Citrus-tone (roughly 5-2-6ish depending on brand) is built for trees, not caneberries. Blackberries also don't need the micronutrient citrus package (iron/zinc/manganese) as much — that's specifically for citrus/avocado chlorosis in alkaline soil, and blackberry doesn't have that same deficiency tendency.
Practical options, ranked:
Just use what you have (citrus-tone) — it'll still work, just won't maximize fruit yield. For one plant, this is genuinely fine and not worth a special trip.
If you want it done right: a balanced berry fertilizer (10-10-10 or similar, sometimes labeled "berry & fruit food") or even a general all-purpose granular (Vigoro All Purpose 10-10-10-ish) would suit it better than citrus-specific.
Compost/aged manure in early spring is honestly what a lot of home blackberry growers rely on more than synthetic fertilizer — blackberries are fairly low-maintenance feeders compared to citrus.
Given you're not chasing commercial yields, I'd just use the citrus-tone you already have at half the label rate and not sweat it further.
